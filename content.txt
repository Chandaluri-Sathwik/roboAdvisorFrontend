For your basic robo-advisor, the four investment options — stocks, mutual funds, commodities, and bonds/debt — are a reasonable starting universe.

The important part is that your questionnaire should tell the backend three things:
1. How much money can the user invest?
2. How much risk can they tolerate?
3. What are they investing for, and when will they need the money?
You don't need a huge questionnaire. I'd start with around 7–9 required inputs, plus a few optional ones.
1. Recommended user input fields

1. Investment amount
Required
Ask how much money the user wants to invest.

Example: ₹10,000, ₹50,000, ₹1,00,000.

Also ask whether it is a one-time investment or a recurring monthly investment (SIP-style).




2. Investment horizon
Required
How long can the user leave the money invested?

Suggested choices:
- Less than 1 year
- 1–3 years
- 3–5 years
- 5–10 years
- More than 10 years
This is important because stocks and commodities can fluctuate significantly over short periods.




3. Risk tolerance
Required
Ask how comfortable the user is with losses or fluctuations.

Suggested choices:
- Low — I prefer stability.
- Moderate — I can accept some fluctuations.
- High — I can tolerate significant fluctuations for potentially higher returns.
This can be used to classify users into conservative, moderate and aggressive profiles.




4. Financial goal
Required
Ask what the user wants to achieve.

Choices: wealth creation, buying a house, education, retirement, or a custom goal.

If they have a specific goal, ask for the target amount and the approximate date they need it.




5. Ability to absorb losses
Required
Ask how financially manageable a temporary loss would be.

Example choices:
- I cannot afford to lose any of this money.
- I could manage a small loss.
- I could manage a moderate loss.
- I can afford a substantial loss.
This is different from risk tolerance: someone may be comfortable with risk but unable to afford a loss.




6. Emergency savings and liquidity needs
Required
Ask whether the user has emergency savings and whether they'll need to withdraw this investment soon.

Example: “Will you need any of this money within the next 12 months?”

Money needed soon should not automatically be allocated to volatile investments.




7. Investment experience
Recommended
Ask whether the user is a beginner, has some experience, or invests regularly.

This helps you explain the recommended portfolio appropriately, although experience alone shouldn't determine risk capacity.




8. Existing investments
Optional
Ask whether the user already has investments in stocks, mutual funds, bonds or other assets.

This helps avoid recommending a portfolio that unintentionally concentrates their overall wealth in one asset class.



2. Should you ask the user for their expected return?
I'd make this optional, rather than a required field.

For example, you could ask for a desired annual return, but a target return is not a guarantee. If someone selects a very high return while having a low risk tolerance and a short investment horizon, your backend should flag the mismatch instead of simply constructing a risky portfolio.

A better approach is to calculate the portfolio's estimated historical return and risk from the stored data, then compare those estimates with the user's goal.

4. How these inputs will affect portfolio recommendations
Your backend can translate the answers into constraints and then calculate a suggested allocation across your four investment categories.
User profile	Likely portfolio behavior
Conservative, short horizon	Emphasize suitable debt instruments; limit volatile assets
Moderate, medium horizon	Balance suitable debt, mutual funds and equities
Aggressive, long horizon	Allow greater equity exposure, subject to loss capacity
Needs money soon	Prioritize liquidity and capital preservation; avoid unsuitable long-term or volatile investments

These are general design principles, not fixed allocation percentages. Commodities should usually be a limited, diversified component rather than a default large allocation. Also, don't assume that every bond or debt instrument is low-risk: credit quality, duration, liquidity and interest-rate changes matter.

My recommendation for your project: start with inputs 1–6 as required, add investment experience as recommended, and make existing investments optional. Avoid asking for age, salary, PAN, bank details or other personal data unless you have a specific, justified need for them.
